# The stage-1 probe at r226: how many lookups a sparse first scan could save

The question: what does `--stage1_probe` say, on the real r226 samples, about cutting k-mer lookups with a
first, sparse scan? The probe and its rules are described in [its report](2026-10-12-stage1-probe/README.md).

## Based on

- The cluster run of `ccdbf52` (SLURM 24193130, q512n10, EPYC 9634, 8 threads, the r226 v24 database on NFS):
  `scripts/measure_performance.sh --stage1_probe` (`7228cce`) over the real paired-end sample PEARL_AGE B10
  (50,607,792 pairs) and a HiFi barcode (497,656 reads), three timed runs each and one probe run. Results in
  `local/Perf0.8.2d` (not in git): `probe.tsv`, `stage1_probe/<sample>_stage1_probe.tsv`, `_stage1_probe_taxa.tsv`,
  `pe1_first_lookups_probe.tsv`, and the probe runs' profiles beside the timed runs'.
- Read with awk on this laptop; nothing was run again. Lookups left by a rule are estimated as 1/c + kept × (1 − 1/c):
  every read's sampled cores, then the rest of the cores of the reads the rule keeps.
- "Recall" is the probe's: the share of reads (pairs as one) with SAM records that the rule keeps. "Called species" are
  the species of the run's profile (592 pe, 196 HiFi), matched to the taxa table by lineage.

## The probe itself

- The profiles with and without `--stage1_probe` are byte-identical for both samples.
- It costs +10.1% wall time on the paired-end sample (124.3 against 112.9 s) and +6.8% on HiFi (38.3 against 35.9 s).
- `measure_performance.sh`'s own summary line for the probe run did not print: in awk, an unparenthesised `>`
  among `printf`'s arguments is read as output redirection, a syntax error. Fixed after `7228cce` (the comparisons
  are made before the `printf`); the other outputs were right.

## Short reads: c = 2 is safe, c = 4 costs 2% of the records

Of the 50.6M pairs, 2.22M (4.4%) have records. Rules with m = 1 (one sampled lookup of score T or more):

| Rule | Pairs kept | Lookups left | Recall | Called species' reads kept | Called species losing > 10% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| c = 1, T = 14 (no sparsity) | 9.92% | 100% | 99.99% | 100.00% | 0 |
| c = 2, T = 14 | 9.40% | 54.7% | 99.63% | 99.71% | 1 |
| c = 2, T = 15 | 8.80% | 54.4% | 99.43% | 99.61% | 2 |
| c = 4, T = 14 | 8.72% | 31.5% | 97.87% | 98.51% | 5 |
| c = 4, T = 13 | 10.03% | 32.5% | 98.05% | 98.63% | 5 |
| c = 8, T = 14 | 8.13% | 19.6% | 92.90% | 94.08% | 67 |
| c = 16, T = 14 | 6.84% | 12.7% | 79.66% | 80.94% | 553 |

- A score threshold alone separates the reads that matter: with every lookup (c = 1), T = 14 keeps 9.9% of the
  pairs and 99.99% of those with records. Sparsity is what costs recall.
- At c = 2 every called species keeps at least 90% of its reads but one. All 29 called species with 5 reads or
  fewer keep all of them, up to c = 2; at c = 4, 27 of 29 do; at c = 8, 23.
- No called species loses half of its reads up to c = 8. The lost reads fall on the called species and the other
  taxa alike: 31-42% of them are of taxa the profile does not call, which hold 17% of the reads with records.
- A pair makes about 44 lookups. At c = 8 it has about 5.5 sampled ones, too few once T = 14 asks for near-exact
  flanks; a long read has hundreds.

## HiFi: c = 8 keeps 99.2% of the records with 28% of the lookups

Of 497,656 reads, 67,569 (13.6%) have records:

| Rule | Reads kept | Lookups left | Recall | Called species' reads kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| c = 4, T = 14 | 21.15% | 40.9% | 99.71% | 99.79% |
| c = 8, T = 14 | 17.90% | 28.2% | 99.17% | 99.42% |
| c = 8, T = 15 | 15.36% | 25.9% | 99.06% | 99.35% |
| c = 16, T = 14 | 16.07% | 21.3% | 97.96% | 98.39% |
| c = 32, T = 14 | 14.52% | 17.2% | 94.71% | 95.40% |

T = 12 or 13 keeps far more reads (60% at c = 8, T = 12) for little recall: 14 is the threshold for long reads too.

## Giving up after a mate's first lookups does not work

`pe1_first_lookups_probe.tsv`: a mate whose first k lookups, in read order, all score s or less would be given up.

| First k lookups, best ≤ s | Mates given up | Aligned mates lost |
|---|---|---|
| k = 4, s = 11 (the log line's rule) | 93.1% | 8.55% |
| k = 8, s = 9 | 47.0% | 2.60% |
| k = 8, s = 8 | 15.2% | 0.86% |
| every lookup, s = 14 | 92.0% | 0.65% |

The first lookups cover one end of the read, which often lies outside the gene or holds an error. The cores of a
hash sample are spread over the whole read, and the same cores are sampled in reads and genes. The last row needs
every lookup, so it saves none; it would only spare the anchoring and alignment of 92% of the mates.

## What it means for an initial scan

The probe supports a sparse-first order within each read, in the same pass and with the same index:

1. Look up a read's (a pair's) cores of level log2(c) or more first.
2. Stop the read unless m of them score T or more.
3. Otherwise look up the rest of its cores, and go on as now.

This needs no second pass over the input, no temporary file and no new database: it saves time, not memory.

| | Rule | Lookups left | Records lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paired-end, safe | c = 2, T = 14, m = 1 | ~55% | 0.37% |
| Paired-end, faster | c = 4, T = 14, m = 1 | ~32% | 2.13% |
| HiFi | c = 8, T = 14, m = 1 | ~28% | 0.83% |

If seeding time scaled with lookups, the paired-end seeding (43.8 s per thread of 89.5 s aligning) would fall by
about 20 s at c = 2 and 30 s at c = 4, before any saving in anchoring for the 90% of pairs stopped. HiFi seeding
(8.7 s per thread) would fall by about 6 s. For HiFi, the larger share of the time is the alignment handler, and how
much of it goes to the reads the rule stops is not measured.

## What the probe cannot tell

- **The cost per lookup.** The stopped reads' lookups may be cheaper or dearer than the kept ones' (blocks of
  different sizes); the estimates above assume the average.
- **The features.** Stopped reads no longer count as failed candidates, so the failed-candidate features change, and
  the models would need retraining on runs with the rule.
- **The calls.** Recall here counts reads with records, not calls. Whether the profile's calls and depths stay within
  noise needs an implementation and the build's test samples.
- **More samples.** This is one sample of each read type.

Next step, if wanted: a development option for the sparse-first order with c and T, then an A/B run on these samples
(seeding time, profiles) and on the r226 build's held-out test samples (F1).
